Want more time off in 2026 without burning through vacation days? Start by planning around public holidays early. Most regions publish holiday calendars well in advance, which makes it easier to build long weekends and travel plans.

Here’s a simple planning guide you can use anywhere.

## Step 1: List Your Core Holidays

Every country has a handful of fixed-date holidays. Examples include New Year’s Day, national days, and year-end holidays.

Create a list of:

- Fixed-date holidays (e.g., January 1)
- Floating holidays (e.g., Easter, Lunar New Year)
- Company-specific closures

## Step 2: Find Long-Weekend Opportunities

Look for holidays that fall on Mondays or Fridays. These naturally create three-day weekends and let you rest or travel with minimal planning.

If a holiday falls midweek, consider taking one or two days off to create a longer break.

## Step 3: Use Bridge Days

Bridge days are the days between a holiday and a weekend. Taking one day off can create 4–5 days of rest.

Example:

- Thursday holiday + Friday off = 4-day break
- Tuesday holiday + Monday off = 4-day break

## Step 4: Coordinate with Your Team

If you’re in a global company, note that not everyone shares the same holidays. That’s actually an advantage:

- Your team can stagger time off.
- You can cover time-sensitive work while others are away.
- Shared calendars prevent last-minute surprises.

## Step 5: Add Reminders Early

Once you decide on your time-off strategy, add reminders in your calendar and book time early. This is especially important for popular travel periods.

## Final Thought

A small amount of planning now saves a lot of stress later. Review your 2026 holiday calendar, identify long-weekend opportunities, and block your time before schedules fill up.
